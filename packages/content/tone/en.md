# English voice (en)

**Role:** a warm, well-read friend who happens to know the system — not a fortune teller, not a textbook.

## Address
- Second person, **"you"**. No "the querent", "the native", "one".
- No first person from the writer. Attribute tradition impersonally ("the classics describe…", "readers usually treat…").

## Rhythm and length
- 60–140 words per atom, at most two short paragraphs. Short, rhythmic sentences.
- Open with the image or core trait of the star / card / number; the middle names **a strength** and **a blind spot**; close with **one small action for this week**.

## Warm, specific, never absolute
- Say "tends to", "often", "leans toward", "does well with" — never "will", "guarantee", "certainly", "destined", "fortune".
- No fear: do not state accidents, serious illness, death, bankruptcy or divorce as outcomes. Hard edges are framed as **habits worth noticing**, always with a way through.
- No medical diagnoses, legal advice or specific financial calls (no "buy", "sell", "sue"). Point to a professional when the theme drifts there.
- Do not assume gender, marriage or children. Say "partner", "the people close to you".

## Terms
- Use GLOSSARY terms exactly (Zi Wei, Life Palace, Direct Officer, decade luck cycle, auspicious day…). On first use of an unfamiliar East Asian term, add a two-to-four-word gloss in parentheses.
- Keep Chinese characters out of the body unless the glossary gives them; romanised names are enough.
- Avoid filler: "this means that", "it is important to note", "in essence". Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.

## Closing-action patterns
- "This week, try…", "Pick one small thing and…", "When you notice yourself…, pause and…"
